Outstation

Outstation cab sharing: a complete guide

25 October 2026 · 4 min read

An outstation cab is one of the most comfortable ways to travel between cities, and one of the priciest when you go alone. Shared with people heading the same way, it costs each of you a fraction of the fare. You don't even need to start at the same place: the cab can pick people up along its route.

How it works

  1. Someone starts the trip: where from, where to, and when.
  2. Others whose trip lies along the same road join. They don't need to start at the same place. Someone across town near the highway fits, and so does someone getting off at a town on the way.
  3. One person books an outstation cab for the whole route, on a ride app or with a local taxi operator.
  4. The cab picks everyone up in order along the road, and drops them in order.
  5. Everyone pays for the distance they ride.

Finding people going your way

For long trips, matching start and end points isn't enough. Two people heading to the same hill station may start 20 km apart, but if the highway passes near both of them, one cab can carry both.

On Sobat, choose Outstation when you search. You'll see rides whose route passes near your pickup and then your drop, even if they start somewhere else. For each ride, Sobat shows when the cab reaches you, how far it would go out of its way to pick you up, and how many kilometres you'd share. Trips of 40 km or more are treated as outstation automatically, and you can switch.

Pickups along the way

  • Choose stops on the main road: a fuel station, a well-known landmark or a bus stop by the highway. Avoid detours into narrow lanes; every extra kilometre is paid for.
  • Agree on times, and be early. If the cab reaches you around 7:40, be there by 7:30. A cab can't wait long on a busy highway.
  • Share your location in the chat as the cab gets close, so the driver doesn't miss you.

Splitting the fare by distance

Everyone pays in proportion to the kilometres they ride. Here's how that works with example numbers.

Example. A 150 km trip costs ₹3,600. Meera rides the whole way (150 km). Arjun joins 30 km in and rides to the end (120 km). Sana rides from the start and gets off at a town 90 km along (90 km). Together: 150 + 120 + 90 = 360 km.

  • Meera: 150 of 360 km → ₹1,500
  • Arjun: 120 of 360 km → ₹1,200
  • Sana: 90 of 360 km → ₹900

Alone, Meera would have paid ₹3,600.

For other ways to split, see how to split a cab fare fairly.

Check these costs before you book

  • Tolls, state entry taxes and parking: on many outstation bookings these are extra. Check what the quoted fare includes.
  • Driver allowance and night charges: some fares add these for late-night or overnight trips.
  • One way or round trip: they're priced differently. If nobody is coming back the same day, book one way.
  • Waiting time: agree how long the cab will wait at each pickup.

Luggage, breaks and comfort

  • Say how many bags you have when you join. A sedan's boot fills up fast with four people's luggage; if everyone has a suitcase, book an SUV.
  • Agree on stops. On a long drive, plan one break for food and toilets so nobody has to ask.
  • Front seat: give it to whoever gets carsick.

Stay safe on longer trips

The same habits apply as in town, with a little more planning: meet at public points, share the trip's live link with someone at home, and keep your phone charged. Our guide to sharing a cab with strangers safely has the full checklist.

How Sobat helps

Outstation rides on Sobat follow the actual road. The ride lists every stop in road order and draws the route, so everyone knows when the cab reaches them. The fare is split by distance automatically, and anyone can adjust their own share. You still book the cab yourselves, and Sobat never handles the money.
