How to split a cab fare fairly

25 October 2026 · 4 min read

The simplest fair split is equal shares: the fare divided by the number of people. When people get off at different places, split by distance instead. Agree on the method before the ride, let one person pay the driver, and settle by UPI as soon as you get out. Worked examples below.

Agree on three things before you get in

  • Who books and pays the driver. One person books on their ride app and pays at the end; everyone else pays them back.
  • How you'll split. Equal shares, or by distance if people get off at different places.
  • What happens with extras: tolls, waiting time and detours (more on these below).

Thirty seconds of agreement at the pickup point saves an awkward conversation at the drop.

Method 1: equal shares

When everyone starts and ends at roughly the same place, split the fare equally per person. If someone brings a friend, they pay two shares.

Round each share to the nearest ₹5 and don't argue over a few rupees.

Method 2: by distance, when drops differ

If one person gets off early, an equal split makes them pay for kilometres they never rode. There are two fair ways to fix that.

Pay in proportion to the distance you ride

Add up everyone's kilometres; each person pays their kilometres' share of the fare.

Example. A ₹600 cab covers 24 km. Asha rides all 24 km, Ben gets off after 16 km and Chirag after 8 km. Together they ride 24 + 16 + 8 = 48 km.

  • Asha: 24 of 48 km, half → ₹300
  • Ben: 16 of 48 km, a third → ₹200
  • Chirag: 8 of 48 km, a sixth → ₹100

Split each stretch among whoever is aboard

Divide the trip into stretches between drops, and share each stretch's cost among the people in the cab for it. With the same ₹600 cab, each 8 km stretch costs ₹200: the first is split three ways, the second two ways, and the last is Asha's alone. Asha pays about ₹367, Ben about ₹167 and Chirag about ₹67.

This second method charges the last rider more, because they ride the final stretch alone. Both are fair; pick one before the ride starts.

Tolls, waiting, detours and surge

  • Tolls: split among the people in the cab when it crosses the toll.
  • Waiting charges: if the cab waited because one person was late, that person covers it.
  • Detours: if the cab goes out of its way for one person's drop, that person pays the extra.
  • Surge pricing: shared equally. Whoever booked would have seen the same price.

Settle up by UPI before you walk away

The booker pays the driver, in the app or in cash. Everyone else sends their share by UPI right there: scan the booker's QR code or send it to their UPI ID. UPI payments between people are free and instant, and some UPI apps have a split-bill feature for groups.

Don't leave it for later. People who share a cab once rarely see each other again, and "I'll send it tonight" is how fares go unpaid.

Who should book?

Anyone in the group can. A good rule: whoever reaches the pickup point first books, so the cab is on its way while the others arrive. Say in the group chat who's booking, so two people don't book two cabs.

How Sobat handles the split

Sobat starts every ride with an equal split by head. Once someone enters what the cab cost, everyone sees their share in rupees. Anyone can change their own share, for example if they get off early, and the chat shows the change so it's never silent. On outstation rides, Sobat splits by the distance each person rides.

Sobat never handles the money: you settle with each other directly.
